Log Viewer — Help

The Log Viewer plots channels from a MoTeC log and overlays what the analysis tools do with it: which stretches the VE Table Tuning filters leave out, and where the Transient Fuel Analyzer finds tip-in and tip-out events. Use it to check that an analysis result comes from data you trust.

Opening a log

Choose a MoTeC .ld log or a MoTeC CSV export. The file is read in your browser and nothing is uploaded. Once it is loaded, the VE filters and transient detection run with the settings under Analysis settings (the same defaults as on those pages); change them and click Re-run analysis.

The same viewer appears on the analysis pages:

  • VE Table Tuning: after processing, click any cell of the results table. The viewer opens below the table with that log and highlights in green the samples that count towards the cell. The bar above the chart shows how many separate stretches there are, their total weight (the cell's hit count) and their mean correction, and Previous / Next step through them.
  • Transient Fuel Analyzer: click an event in the tip-in or tip-out table to zoom to it.

Both use exactly the settings the page's analysis ran with, so the counts in the viewer match the page's results.

Channels and layouts

  • Fuelling layout: lambda (both banks) with Fuel Mixture Aim, closed-loop trims, MAP, engine speed and throttle.
  • Transient layout: lambda with aim, MAP, throttle, Engine Overrun State and Fuel Output Cut Count.
  • My layout: your own. Type a channel name into Add a channel… (the list offers every channel in the log), choose a pane or a new pane, and click Add. Remove a channel with its ×. Your layout is remembered in this browser, separately for each page.

Each channel shows its logging rate. In many M1 logs lambda, MAP and throttle are logged at 10 Hz while engine speed is 100 Hz; the analysis tools hold each slow value until the next one arrives, and so does the chart.

Channels in a pane that share a unit share one scale, with values on the left. Channels with different units each scale to the pane, and their range is shown in the legend.

VE filter bands

Shaded bands mark the samples the VE analysis leaves out, coloured by the reason. Each legend chip gives the number of samples; click a chip to hide or show that band. Unshaded time is used.

Transient events

Each detected event is a dashed line at its start (cyan for tip-in, amber for tip-out) with a shaded measuring window, which runs until 1.5 s after MAP settles. The label gives the lean spike or rich overshoot. Grey lines mark events skipped because they overlap overrun or a fuel cut. The table below the chart lists all of them in time order; click a row to zoom to it.

What to look for: lambda should only swing while the throttle or MAP is moving. If a band or event looks wrong, for example a lambda spike that isn't shaded or an event lined up with a fuel cut, note the time and the settings used.

Troubleshooting

  • "VE filters" shows an error: the log is missing a channel the VE analysis needs (Engine Speed, Inlet Manifold Pressure, Fuel Mixture Aim, and a lambda channel). The chart still works.
  • No transient events: the log needs Inlet Manifold Pressure and a lambda channel. Events also need a warm engine (Transient: Min coolant).
  • "Time" header row not found: the CSV isn't a MoTeC export (for example a Haltech log).
