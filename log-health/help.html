Log Health — Help

Every analysis tool assumes the log's readings are true. A wrong sensor translation, a failing sensor, a value the ECU substituted, or a log that joins two drives produces confident but wrong VE, fuel and timing advice. Log Health looks for readings that can't be right and says what it measured and over how much of the log. Run it first on every new log.

Choose the tune that ran the log (it is suggested from the log's ECU serial and time when tunes are stored). With it, diagnostic values get the firmware's own names and severities, and the fuel model checks run.

Results

  • Fail — a reading that can't be physically right, or a fault the firmware tags as one. Fix before tuning from this log.
  • Check — implausible or suspicious; look at it in the log.
  • Note — worth knowing, not wrong in itself (e.g. fuel cut states, no key-on data).
  • OK — checked and plausible, with what was measured.

Show in log opens the embedded Log Viewer with the channels behind the finding, the stretches marked and the first one zoomed.

What is checked

  • Log — holds (Engine Speed, logged at 100 Hz, exactly unchanged while running means the logger stopped). If temperatures jump across the hold, the log joins two key cycles with the engine off in between; if the engine was cranking right after, it was restarted. Stalls and near-stalls while logging. Readings around holds and the first seconds after each start are left out of the other checks.
  • ECU diagnostics — every Diagnostic channel; any value other than OK / Not in Use, with its time, duration and the firmware's name and severity (fault / warning / information).
  • Substituted values — "X Sensor" (the reading) against "X" (the value the ECU uses): a lasting difference means a sensor diagnostic made the ECU fall back to an estimate or default.
  • Stuck sensors — sensor channels that never change while the engine runs.
  • Pressure sensors — key-on engine-off MAP, airbox and ambient all reading the same plausible barometric pressure; Ambient Pressure in 70–108 kPa; MAP never ≤ 0 (or below 8 kPa) while running; MAP and Airbox Pressure agreeing at part load (the throttle is ahead of the supercharger); oil pressure ~0 with the engine stopped and above 140 kPa on a warm engine above 2000 rpm.
  • Temperatures — outside −35…145 °C (open/short circuit), jumps faster than 15 °C/s (intermittent connection), sensors agreeing at a cold start, coolant above 108 °C, inlet air temperature under boost.
  • Lambda — a bank reading free air or nothing while fuelling (after sensor warm-up), bank 1 vs bank 2 agreement, closed-loop trims sitting at their limits.
  • Fuel model (needs the tune) — logged stoich against the tune's fuel properties and the fuel composition; whether the alternative fuel is set up as pure ethanol or an E85-like blend, and what that means for stoich if the sensor reports ethanol content; where Fuel Mixture Aim comes from (Main, the alternative fuel blend, or a compensation). Composition moving during a drive.
  • Fuel supply — low-side pressure below 250 kPa, sagging at high fuel flow, above the tune's warning limit; gauge pressures below −20 kPa; direct injection rail pressure missing.
  • Throttle — position more than 5 % off its aim for 0.5 s or more.
  • Electrical — battery voltage while running and dips below 12 V, PDM vs ECU battery voltage (wiring drop), injector boost supply.
  • Knock inputs — a cylinder with no knock signal, one bank reading several times lower than the other.
  • Speed signals — engine speed jumps (lost teeth), wheel speeds disagreeing or dropping to 0 while moving.
Log 5 s with the key on before starting the engine: that gives the barometric check (MAP, airbox and ambient against each other) and the cold-start temperature comparison.
