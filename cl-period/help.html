Closed-Loop Period — Help

Fuel Closed Loop Period (by Exhaust Mass Flow) is the time from a change in fuelling to the lambda sensor starting to see it — the transport delay. The closed loop waits that long before judging its last correction. Too long and the control is lazy: trims lag and lambda wanders off aim. Too short and it is hyperactive: it corrects again before the first correction arrives, and lambda oscillates (MoTeC training 39). Set the Period first, then the trim gain (training 40). VE Table Tuning's "from the tune" lambda delay also uses this table.

The step test (MoTeC's method)

  1. Log Exhaust Lambda Bank 1 / 2 and Fuel Mixture Aim as fast as the logger allows, with Engine Speed, Inlet Manifold Pressure, Engine Load, Fuel Stoichiometric Ratio and Engine Overrun State. MoTeC sets Fuel Closed Loop Control Trim Minimum / Maximum to 0 so the trim can't act; the onset is measurable with the trim on too, since it only reacts after the Period.
  2. Hold a steady point near one exhaust flow site (the findings say which rpm × MAP the logs reached each site at).
  3. In Fuel Mixture Aim Main, change the cell you are in by −0.1 λ (e.g. 1.00 → 0.90). Hold 3 s. Put it back. Hold 3 s. Three times.
  4. Repeat at the other flow sites, then load the log(s) here.

An aim step counts when the aim moves ≥ 0.03 λ within 0.25 s, holds 1 s before and 1.5 s after, with rpm within 250 and MAP within 8 kPa, no overrun cut and the engine running ≥ 30 s. Its delay is the time until lambda has moved a quarter of the step (at least 0.01 λ); the sensor must reach half the step within 2 s or the step is not counted for that bank.

Overrun cuts as natural steps

An overrun fuel cut is a step to no fuel. A cut counts when lambda was within 0.08 λ of aim 0.5 s and 0.05 s before it, the cut lasted ≥ 0.8 s, above 1200 rpm, engine warm. Its delay is the time until lambda reads 0.1 λ leaner. The cut state is logged at 10 Hz, so the real cut can be up to 100 ms before the logged one: suggestions from cuts add 50 ms. Cuts happen at low exhaust flow (closed throttle), so they only measure the low sites.

Suggestions

  • Steps are grouped at the nearest Period site. A site needs 3 aim steps or 4 clean cuts, and the delays' p10–p90 within 150 ms.
  • Suggested = the median delay (+50 ms from cuts), rounded up to 50 ms — a slightly long Period is the safe side. Differences under 100 ms are left alone.
  • Sites without measurements keep their value; blend them toward the measured ones by hand. Copy Period row copies the whole row for M1 Tune.
  • With lambda logged at 10 Hz, one step is timed to about ±50 ms; several steps per site average that out.
