DI Injection Timing — Help

With direct injection the fuel is sprayed into the cylinder, so it has to go in while the exhaust valve is shut and be finished well before the spark. The page works out that window from the cam, recommends a start of injection, and checks the tune's timing table, its limits and a log against it.

The angle scale

Everything is in M1's dBTDC — degrees before the firing TDC — as M1 Tune shows Fuel Timing Primary Main and its limits. A larger number is earlier in the cycle.

dBTDCPoint in the cycle
720 / 0Firing TDC (end of compression, start of power)
540BDC after the power stroke (exhaust stroke begins)
360TDC between exhaust and intake (overlap TDC)
180BDC after the intake stroke (compression begins)

The MoTeC training spreadsheet uses a 720° scale counted from firing TDC instead; the page shows it too (it is 720 − dBTDC). An event "26° after overlap TDC" is 334 dBTDC, or 386° on the spreadsheet's scale.

Cam inputs

  • Durations from the cam card, normally at 0.050" lift. The page converts them to seat-to-seat (0.006") by adding 52°, as the GM Gen V calculator does; if your card lists 0.006" (advertised) durations, choose that instead, and change the 52° if your cam maker gives a different figure.
  • Lobe separation and intake centreline (as installed). The exhaust centreline is 2 × LSA − ICL.
  • Cam phaser advance: degrees the phaser has advanced the cam (filled from the tune's Inlet Camshaft Aim). On a cam-in-block engine such as the GM Gen V the phaser turns the whole cam, so tick Phaser moves the exhaust too.
Why seat to seat: fuel can escape whenever the exhaust valve is off its seat, even below 0.050" lift, so the window is judged on the 0.006" events.

Recommended start of injection

Halfway between the intake valve opening and the intake centreline (seat-to-seat events), but no earlier than 20° after overlap TDC — the method of the GM Gen V calculator — and never earlier than 5° after the exhaust valve seats. Use it across Fuel Timing Primary Main as a starting point (M1's edge for DI is start of injection). Then fine-tune at steady load: move it a few degrees at a time and keep the setting where lambda goes richest without a VE change, which is where the fuel mixes best. With a stock cam, GM's own timing is already close.

Limits

  • Injection Start Limit — the earliest M1 will start injecting; table cells earlier than the limit start at the limit. Ideally after the exhaust valve seats (the page suggests about 5° after). On a supercharged or turbocharged engine an earlier start can be deliberate at high rpm and load, to keep a long pulse out of the compression stroke — at the cost of some fuel through the exhaust — so allow only as much as those cells need.
  • Primary Limit Main — the latest a pulse may end; a longer pulse is cut short (the engine runs lean there). Keep it at least 10° before the most advanced spark (GM allows 5°): fuel sprayed into the spark can douse it and misfire.
  • Limit Margin is shown from the tune for reference only.

Why the window matters

  • Too early (exhaust valve still open): fuel goes straight out of the exhaust.
  • Too late in the intake stroke (near BDC): the air is coming in slowly, so the fuel mixes poorly. The tune review flags cells later than 230 dBTDC.
  • Into the compression stroke: rising cylinder pressure cuts the injector's flow (the rail pressure drops in the log) and the fuel puddles instead of mixing — sooting, misfire and lost power even with lambda on target.
  • Near the spark: the fuel douses the spark and misfires.

With pulses of a few milliseconds the window is short: around 6 ms is the practical limit at high rpm.

Start of injection by RPM

For the longest pulse width you expect: where that pulse ends at each RPM, and the longest pulse that fits before BDC and before the Primary Limit. Where the pulse would reach the compression stroke the start is moved earlier, but never before the exhaust valve seats. With a log loaded, the longest pulse is set from it (+10%) unless you type a value — but a log without full-throttle pulls at the top of the rev range under-states it.

Tune review

With a tune (a stored tune from the M1 Config Viewer, suggested from the log's date and ECU, or a .m1pkg), the page fills the limits, the most spark advance (from Ignition Timing Main), the cam phaser and the RPM sites, and shows Fuel Timing Primary Main: red cells start before the exhaust valve seats. With a log too, each cell shows its longest pulse and latest end of injection — orange where the injection reaches the compression stroke, red where it runs past the Primary Limit.

Log check

Uses the logged start angle (Fuel Cylinder 1 Primary Output Pulse Angle), pulse width and Ignition Timing for every reading with the engine running, and reports any injection that started before the exhaust valve seats, ran past the Primary Limit or ended within 10° of the spark, plus the latest end of injection in the log.

Troubleshooting

Raw fuel smell from the exhaust

Injection is starting while the exhaust valve is still open. Make the start of injection later — a smaller dBTDC number — and check the Injection Start Limit is after the seat-to-seat EVC.

Lean, sooting, misfiring or down on power at high RPM and load

The pulse is probably running into the compression stroke, past the Primary Limit (cut short) or close to the spark. Check the log's latest end of injection. The best fix is more rail pressure, which shortens the pulse for the same fuel (if the pumps can supply it). Otherwise start injection earlier — a larger number — even before the exhaust valve seats where that is what it takes. Split injection is for cranking; it does not help at high rpm, where the window is already short.
