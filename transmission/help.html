Transmission Shift Analyzer — Help

For a ZF 8HP on a TurboLamik TCU. It reads a TunerPro RT log of the TCU, times every shift from the moment it is requested to the moment the new gear is in, looks for flare (slip) and bumps, and checks whether the TCU's clutch adaptation could learn. With the TCU bin it also reads the gear ratios, start gear, blip limits and the correction tables, and names the tables to look at for each finding.

Channels

  • Required: Gear.
  • For full shift timing: Gear Target, Input RPM, Output RPM. Without Gear Target the delay before the ratio moves can't be measured; without Input / Output RPM, neither can the shift phases, flare and feel.
  • Also used: Engine RPM, TPS, Engine TQ (Nm), Oil Temp (C), Vehicle Speed (km/h), Time 1 Shift Time, Foot Brake, Blip Status, TQ Cut 1/2 Flag, Adaptation Activation Parameter, Program Sw., Upshift Mode, Lockup Slip %, and the Clutch 1-A … 5-D Pres. Adaptation channels.

How a Shift Is Timed

The TCU changes Gear Target when it requests a shift and changes Gear only once the new ratio (Input ÷ Output RPM) has been reached — on an upshift about a second later. So each shift runs from the Gear Target change to the Gear change, in two parts:

  • Delay: request until the ratio has moved more than 2 % from the old gear's. This is the oncoming element filling and taking torque.
  • Ratio change: until the ratio is within 1.5 % of the new gear's for 0.1 s. The TCU's own Time 1 Shift Time counts this part and is shown next to it as a check.

All times come from the log's timestamps. TunerPro writes rows in pairs with the same time; only one row per timestamp is used. Gear ratios are measured from the log; a gear that isn't held long enough takes the bin's ratio, or the standard 8HP ratio without a bin.

Requests that are cancelled before Gear changes, take longer than 5 s, or happen with Output RPM under 150 (too coarse to measure) are counted in the Summary and left out of the timing.

Shift Feel

  • Flare: input speed above where it should be held — on an upshift above the old gear's speed (Output RPM × ratio), on a downshift above the new gear's. The highest excess held for 0.1 s; flagged from 100 rpm.
  • Sag / bump: vehicle acceleration worked out from Output RPM (slope over 0.25 s, scaled to km/h with the logged vehicle speed), against its median in the 0.5 s before the request. Sag is the drop during the shift, bump the rise at the catch; bumps are flagged from 0.15 g. Braking shifts are left out of the findings because the brake moves the acceleration more than the shift does.
  • Blip and torque cut: whether the TCU asked for them during the shift.

Results

Findings

Only patterns that repeat: a finding needs at least 2 shifts of that kind and at least half of that transition's shifts. Flare, bump and a slow start (more than 0.8 s before the ratio moves, upshifts) are checked per transition. Each finding names the tables TurboLamik's shift-quality guide points to — Pulse Correction, Time Correction with Slow Ram, and that shift's Correction Start / End. These thresholds are this tool's, not TurboLamik's.

Summary

Shift counts, log length, oil temperature range at the shifts, gears used, the start gear from the bin, and the measured gear ratios.

Shifts by Transition and All Shifts

Medians per gear change (delay, ratio change, total, TCU shift time, flare, sag, bump, oil, blip, type), then every shift. Click a shift in the list to chart it; the buttons show all, upshifts or downshifts.

Shift Elements

The 8HP applies three of its five elements (A–E) in each gear, and each shift hands over from one to another. Results are grouped by the oncoming element, so a weak element shows up across every shift that applies it.

Adaptation

The TCU learns clutch pressure adaptation on five shifts only: 3→4 (D), 4→5 (C), 5→6 (E), 6→7 (A) upshifts and the 6→5 (B) downshift. The section shows how long the Adaptation Activation Parameter spent on each code, the code at each of those shifts, and how the logged clutch adaptation values moved from the start to the end of the log.

Codes are from the official TurboLamik manual (manual.turbolamik.eu), which the logs match; the community guide (turbolamik.help) lists them differently. The manual warns the limits can change between firmware versions.

To let it learn: oil 55–80 °C (or the bin's Oil Temp MIN / Max), Program 1, 1600–3200 rpm, 55–180 Nm, light steady throttle through those shifts, about 20 times.

Bin Settings

With a bin: gear ratios, start gear per program (Program Selector, ST. GEAR row), the adaptation oil window, blip limits (Min RPM for blip, Max TPS / TQ for blip, Clutch Slip Blip Deactivation, Blip Out), the Correction Start / End tables for the shifts in the log, and the shift-feel tables (Pulse / Time Correction, Slow Ram, Gear Pulse Correction %, Corr. Temp.).

Judge shift quality with warm oil. Below the adaptation window the TCU adds its cold corrections (Corr. Temp. Gear UP / DOWN, Corr. Temp. Pulse Time), so cold shifts show cold behaviour. If every shift in the log was cold, the tool says so first. Change base shift tables only from a warm-oil log; problems that happen only cold belong in the temperature tables.
Start gear 2: if the bin's start gear is 2 in every program, 1st isn't used, and the Summary says so — a log with no 1st gear is then expected, not a fault.
