Charge Cooling Gain — Help

The charge cooling gain represents how much the injected fuel cools the intake charge before combustion. It directly affects the air density calculation in the MoTeC airflow model.

What is Charge Cooling?

When fuel is injected it absorbs heat as it evaporates, so the charge entering the cylinder is cooler than the inlet air sensor reads. Engine Charge Cooling Gain tells the M1 how much cooler. With the charge temperature corrections zeroed, the charge temperature is the inlet air temperature minus the gain — in MoTeC's training, 26.6 °C inlet air with a 13 °C gain gives a 13.6 °C charge temperature. A cooler charge is denser, so the model puts in more fuel.

The gain matters when the amount of fuel changes. On a step to a richer aim, a gain that is too low under-estimates the extra cooling, so the model under-fuels: lambda stays leaner than the new aim. A gain that is too high over-fuels: lambda goes richer than the aim.

Prerequisites

  1. Idle VE tuned — at the starting aim (1.0) lambda should match. The tool compares the change in lambda with the change in aim, so a small base error cancels out, but tune the idle VE first as the training does.
  2. Closed loop off — disable it, or zero its trim minimum and maximum. The tool rejects any step where the logged trims move more than 0.5 %.
  3. Charge temperature simplified — Engine Charge Temperature Time Constant, Port Temperature Time Constant and the Correction Factor table at 0 (and Inlet Manifold Temperature mode disabled), as in the training.
  4. Engine warm and idling steadily — the tool rejects steps where engine speed moves more than 150 rpm or manifold pressure more than 5 kPa.

Test Procedure

  1. Warm the engine; idle out of gear.
  2. Turn closed loop off.
  3. Log Fuel Mixture Aim, Exhaust Lambda (or both banks), the closed-loop trims, Engine Speed, Inlet Manifold Pressure and Inlet Air Temperature.
  4. Change the aim at the idle site from 1.00 to 0.90 and hold 5–10 s, then back to 1.00 and hold 5–10 s.
  5. Repeat two or three times, then load the log here.
  6. Change the gain in the direction shown (about 2 °C at a time) and repeat until lambda tracks within about 0.01.
Tip: a step leaner (1.00 → 1.05) works too; the tool reads each step the right way round. Any change of the aim by 0.03 or more between two stretches where it holds still for 3 s counts as a step.

Interpreting Results

For each step the tool averages lambda over the last 2 s before it and from 1.5 s after it (the sensor's delay) to the end of the step, and works out the tracking error: how far the change in lambda differs from the change in aim.

Within 0.5 %

The gain is right.

Rich step stays leaner than the aim

Example: aim 1.00 → 0.90, lambda 1.00 → 0.94. The gain is too low — raise it.

Rich step goes richer than the aim

Example: aim 1.00 → 0.90, lambda 1.00 → 0.88. The gain is too high — lower it.

The tool doesn't predict the exact value: the training changes it about 2 °C at a time and re-tests. If you enter the current gain it shows the next value to try.

Fuel and Inlet Air Temperature

Ethanol absorbs much more heat than petrol, so it needs a higher gain; the gain table can have a fuel composition axis for flex fuel, and each composition has to be tested. The table also runs on inlet air temperature: a test sets the value near the temperature it was done at, so check again with hot inlet air.

Heat soak: an inlet air sensor in the manifold heat-soaks at idle and reads high, which throws off the test. A sensor after the intercooler or in the charge pipe avoids it; otherwise test straight after a short drive.

Troubleshooting

No step test in this log

In normal driving the aim moves gradually with load, so there is nothing to test. Change the aim abruptly in the table and hold it.

Steps found but none usable

  • Closed loop active — the trims moved more than 0.5 %; turn closed loop off.
  • Not steady — engine speed or manifold pressure moved during the step (idle hunting, fan or A/C switching).
  • Less than 1 s of valid lambda — hold each step longer.
